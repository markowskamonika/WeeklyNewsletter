📨 Newsletter AI & Automation

This week’s updates focus on AI systems being used for research rather than only routine office tasks. OpenAI described applications in mathematical proof, internal research measurement, and laboratory automation for quantum computing experiments. For readers building practical workflows, these examples show how automation can support specialized work while leaving human experts responsible for judgment and verification.

🔥 Hot news

On the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize Problem

OpenAI says an internal AI system produced a solution to the Navier–Stokes existence-and-smoothness problem, a long-standing Millennium Prize Problem. The company released a proof write-up and a formalization in Lean, a tool that represents mathematical proofs in a machine-checkable format, while claiming that smooth fluid dynamics can develop a finite-time singularity. The practical implication is that AI-generated mathematical results can be examined through both written arguments and formal verification tools.

🔗 openai.com | 2026-09-08

How GPT-5.6 Sol helps run quantum computing experiments

MIT graduate student Beatriz Yankelevich connected GPT-5.6 Sol through Codex to laboratory software used for superconducting-qubit experiments. The agent ran and refined routine measurements, giving the researcher more time for experimental design, interpretation, and planning. For technical teams, this shows how an AI agent can be connected to specialized software to handle repeatable research procedures rather than working only through a chat interface.

🔗 openai.com | 2026-09-08

Research acceleration: The view inside OpenAI

OpenAI described how it uses increasingly capable AI systems to accelerate internal research and argued that the public needs a clearer understanding of these capabilities for governance. The post also discusses the difficulty of measuring progress from agent-powered research, including challenges in defining useful metrics and designing reliable methods. The practical implication is that organizations adopting research agents should track measurable outcomes and document how those results were produced.
