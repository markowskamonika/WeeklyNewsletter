📨 Newsletter AI & Automation

This week’s updates focus on more capable AI models and the infrastructure needed to use them in business processes. OpenAI introduced a new image-generation model and described broader applications for advanced agents, while enterprise automation news highlighted zero-copy data access and AI-powered IT support.

🔥 Hot news

Introducing ChatGPT Images 2.5

OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Images 2.5, an image-generation model designed to produce sharper details, generate images faster, and support more precise editing. The release also adds GPT-Image-2.5 Flare and the higher-precision Sunburst model to the API, with C2PA metadata and invisible watermarking safeguards. For practical use, creative and marketing automation builders can evaluate these models through ChatGPT and the API.

🔗 openai.com | 2026-09-08

The Work Now Within Reach

OpenAI outlined how increasingly capable models could expand the range of economically viable work for individuals and businesses. The post highlights GPT-6 Astra’s capabilities in computer use, browsing, software engineering, cybersecurity, science, and professional tasks, as well as OpenAI’s internal use of agents in research workflows. For teams exploring agentic automation, the practical implication is that more knowledge-work tasks may be suitable for structured experiments with AI agents.

🔗 openai.com | 2026-09-08

Palo Alto Networks paid $500M for Thrive-backed Console, sources say

Palo Alto Networks acquired Console for roughly $500 million in cash and stock, according to sources cited by TechCrunch. Console uses AI agents to automate routine IT help-desk work, making the deal another signal of enterprise interest in agent-led IT operations. For automation teams, the practical implication is that service-desk workflows remain an important area for evaluating AI-based task handling.

🔗 techcrunch.com | 2026-09-02

🤖 RPA & BPA

Why business orchestration and automation need a zero-copy data layer

UiPath’s Shivaraman Shankar argues that repeatedly copying enterprise data creates latency, inconsistency, and governance problems for automation. The article presents Data Fabric’s zero-copy approach, which gives agents, robots, workflows, and applications access to governed live or near-real-time enterprise context without repeatedly duplicating the data. For RPA and BPA projects, this highlights the importance of reliable, current data access when scaling beyond isolated workflows.

🔗 uipath.com | 2026-09-08
